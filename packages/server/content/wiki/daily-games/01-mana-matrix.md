---
title: Mana Matrix
description: Name a card for every cell of a 3×3 grid of card categories.
---

Mana Matrix is a grid puzzle. Three categories run across the top and three down the side, and
each of the nine cells asks for a card that fits both its row and its column. If a row says
"power is 2" and a column says "type line includes Faerie", any 2-power Faerie solves that cell.
Play it at [cubecobra.com/manamatrix](/manamatrix), or from **Dailies** in the **Explore** menu.

## The categories

Each day's six categories are drawn from a mix of card properties: a creature type, a mana value,
a number of colors in the color identity, an exact color identity, a card type, a power, a
toughness, a keyword ability, a mana cost, a set, an oracle tag, an art tag, or a phrase from rules
text (like "destroy target"). No two categories in a puzzle are the same kind, except rules-text
phrases, which can fill up to three of the six slots.

On a computer, the labels read in plain English; on a phone, where space is tight, they show the
search syntax instead (`power=2`, for example). Click or tap any label to open a **Category**
window with both versions. The syntax is the same one card search uses, so
[Filter Syntax](/wiki/reference/filter-syntax) explains what each label matches.

An empty cell shows how many cards fit it. Every cell has at least five valid answers, and most
have far more.

## Filling in a cell

Click a cell to open the **Name a card** window, which restates what the cell needs. Start typing
and pick a name from the suggestions; anything that isn't a real card name gets "No card by that
name — pick one from the suggestions." The suggestions don't show card images, so they can't give
away whether a card fits. **Save** puts the card in the cell (with its art as the tile
background), and saving an empty box clears the cell.

What counts as a valid answer:

- Any printing of the card can satisfy the categories. When a category depends on the printing,
  like a set or an art tag, a single printing has to satisfy both of the cell's categories.
- Tokens, digital-only cards, and other non-standard cards don't count.
- The same card can fill more than one cell, as long as it fits each one.

## Checking your answers

Fill in as many cells as you like, then press **Check your score!** to grade every filled cell at
once. Correct cells get a green check and lock in; wrong ones get a red X. Change any wrong cell
and press **Check again!** to grade the board again. Cells you've already solved stay solved no
matter what happens later.

There's no limit on checks. Each one adds one to the **Guesses** counter above the grid, and your
result is how many cells you solved and how many guesses it took. The counter beside it shows
**Filled** cells before your first check and **Solved** cells after. Once all nine are solved, the
check button goes away.

Logged in, your board is saved each time you check it; answers you've typed in but not checked are
lost if you leave the page. Anonymous, your board is kept in your browser as you go.

## Answer popularity

Each solved cell shows how common your answer was, like "12% answered this (3/25)": of the 25
logged-in players who solved that cell, 3 named the same card you did. Picking an uncommon card
doesn't change your score; it's there for comparison.

Only logged-in players' answers are counted, and only once per player per cell: the answer that
first solved it. Anonymous players still see the percentages, but their answers aren't added.

## Results and sharing

After your first check, a results strip appears under the grid: "6 out of 9 correct.", followed on
today's puzzle by a short verdict. Three buttons sit under it:

- **Copy results** copies a text version for sharing: the date, a 3×3 grid of correct, wrong, and
  empty marks, how many attempts you took, and a link to the game.
- **Copy image** copies a picture of your board, with your answers and their popularity. If your
  browser can't copy images, it downloads the picture instead.
- **Community answers** opens a window with a small copy of the board. Pick a cell to see every
  card that fits it, most-named first, with how many players named each one and what share of the
  cell's answers that is. It's available from your first check on, and it lists every valid
  answer, so opening it before you're done gives the rest of the board away.

## Stats

Logged-in players get four stats, shown under the results strip on today's puzzle and at the top
of the archive:

- **Current Streak** and **Best Streak** count consecutive days you've checked the day's puzzle at
  least once. Solving it isn't required. See
  [Streaks and stats](/wiki/daily-games#streaks-and-stats) for the rules all three puzzle games
  share.
- **Days Played** counts every puzzle you've checked at least once, archive puzzles included.
- **Perfect Days** counts puzzles where you solved all nine cells, however many guesses it took.

## The archive

The **Archive** link at the top of the game leads to the Mana Matrix Archive: every puzzle so far,
newest first, 30 at a time. Each row shows the date and the board. If you've played that day, the
board shows your answers (green borders for solved cells, red for misses) and a line like "6/9 in
3 guesses" beside a small dot map of which cells you got. Otherwise the row says **Play →**.

Opening a past puzzle plays it normally, marked "archive puzzle" beside its date. It counts toward
Days Played and Perfect Days but not your streak, and its results strip leaves off the verdict and
your stats.
